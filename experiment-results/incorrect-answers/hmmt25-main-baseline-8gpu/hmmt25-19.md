In a **regular hexagon**, there are **three pairs of opposite edges**. Each pair consists of two **parallel edges** (e.g., top and bottom, left and right, etc.). The key is to determine when the **line segment** between two randomly chosen interior points **intersects both edges** of one such pair.

This occurs precisely when the **two points lie on opposite sides of the midline** (the line equidistant from the two parallel edges).

- If the horizontal pair of edges is at $ y = \pm \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} $, then the midline is $ y = 0 $. A line intersects both horizontal edges **only if** the two points lie on **opposite sides of $ y = 0 $**.

### 🧠 Key Insight: Symmetry and Strips

The regular hexagon has **three directions** of parallel edges (horizontal, diagonal with slope 1, diagonal with slope -1). For each direction, we can define a **strip** of the hexagon where lines can intersect both edges of that pair.

Due to the **symmetry** of the regular hexagon:
- Each of these three strips has **equal area**.
- Within each strip, the **midline** divides it into two equal halves.
- A line intersects the corresponding pair of opposite edges **if and only if** the two points lie in **opposite halves** of the same strip.

- $ A $: total area of the hexagon
- $ A_{\text{strip}} = \frac{2}{3}A $: area of one strip
- $ A_{\text{half}} = \frac{1}{3}A $: area of one half of a strip
